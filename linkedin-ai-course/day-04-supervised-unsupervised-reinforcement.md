Day 4/30 — Supervised vs unsupervised vs reinforcement learning

---

There are three main ways to teach a machine, and they map surprisingly well
onto how humans learn too.

→ Supervised learning: learning from labeled examples with a known right
answer. Like flashcards — "here's the input, here's the correct output,
learn the pattern." Used for spam detection, price prediction, medical
image diagnosis.

→ Unsupervised learning: finding structure in data with no right answers
given. Like being handed a messy pile of photos and asked to sort them into
groups without being told what the groups are. Used for customer
segmentation, anomaly detection.

→ Reinforcement learning: learning through trial, error, and reward — no
labeled data at all, just a signal for "good" or "bad" outcomes. Like
training a dog with treats. Used for game-playing AI, robotics, and — this
part surprises people — a key step in training chatbots to be helpful
(RLHF: reinforcement learning from human feedback).

Most real products blend more than one of these. A recommendation engine
might use unsupervised learning to group similar users, then supervised
learning to predict what each group clicks on.

Which of these three feels most intuitive to you, and which still feels
like a black box?
