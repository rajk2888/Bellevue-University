Day 1/30 — What is AI, really?
---

Everyone says "AI" like it's one thing. It isn't.

For most of my career, "AI" meant a chatbot or a sci-fi robot. Then I actually
sat down and learned how this stuff works, and the definition got a lot more
boring — in the best way.

AI is just software that makes decisions by finding patterns in data instead
of following rules a human wrote line by line. A spam filter, a Netflix
recommendation, a self-driving car's lane detector, and ChatGPT are all "AI."
They just sit at very different points on a spectrum of complexity.

That reframe changed how I read AI news. Instead of "is this magic or a
threat," I started asking: what pattern is this system trained to find, and
what data did it learn that pattern from? Ninety percent of the confusion
about AI disappears once you ask that question.

Starting today, I'm posting one AI concept a day for the next 30 days —
building from fundamentals up to how these systems actually get built and
shipped. No hype, no jargon left unexplained.

If you could finally understand one thing about AI, what would it be? Drop
it in the comments — I'll make sure it's covered somewhere in this series.
