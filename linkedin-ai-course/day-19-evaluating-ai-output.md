Day 19/30 — Evaluating AI output: how do you know it's good?

---

"That looks right" is not an evaluation strategy — especially once you're
using AI for anything repeated or high-stakes. Here's how to actually check
your work.

For one-off tasks, a simple checklist works:
→ Factual claims — verify anything checkable (Day 9 territory) against a
real source.
→ Completeness — did it address every part of your prompt, or quietly
drop a requirement?
→ Fit for purpose — is the tone, length, and format actually usable as-is,
or does it need editing?

For repeated tasks — anything you're running the same prompt on
regularly — build a small evaluation set: a handful of representative test
cases with a rough sense of what "good" looks like for each. Run your
prompt against them whenever you change it, and compare. This turns
"does this new prompt work better?" from a vibe into a measurement.

For anything genuinely high-stakes — medical, legal, financial, safety —
the standard bar is: a qualified human reviews the output before it's
acted on. AI accelerates the draft; it shouldn't replace the sign-off.

The teams getting the most value from AI right now aren't the ones using
it the most — they're the ones who've built the tightest feedback loop for
checking whether it's actually working.
