Day 3/30 — How machines "learn": training data explained simply

---

The word "learn" makes it sound like the computer is thinking. It's really
just adjusting numbers until it stops being wrong as often.

Here's the simplified version: you show the model thousands (or billions) of
examples — an email and whether it's spam, a photo and what's in it, a
sentence and the next word. The model guesses. If it's wrong, an algorithm
nudges its internal parameters slightly so it's more likely to get that
example right next time. Repeat that process across the entire dataset,
many times over, and the model's guesses get better and better.

That's it. That's "learning." No understanding required — just relentless,
automated trial and error at a scale no human could do by hand.

The part that actually matters isn't the algorithm — it's the data. A model
trained on biased, incomplete, or low-quality data will confidently produce
biased, incomplete, low-quality answers. "Garbage in, garbage out" is the
single most important sentence in this entire field.

Next time you evaluate an AI product, ask what data it was trained on before
you ask what algorithm it uses. The data question matters more.
