Day 10/30 — AI ethics & bias: the human side of the machine

---

Ten days into this series, one theme keeps resurfacing: AI systems inherit
everything from their data — including our flaws.

If training data (Day 3) underrepresents a group, the model performs worse
for that group. If historical data encodes past discrimination — biased
hiring decisions, biased lending data — a model trained on it can learn and
automate that same bias, just faster and at greater scale. This isn't
speculative; it's been documented repeatedly in hiring tools, facial
recognition, and credit scoring systems.

Bias isn't the only ethical thread, either:
→ Consent — was the data the model was trained on used with permission?
→ Transparency — do users know they're interacting with AI, and how
decisions get made?
→ Accountability — when an AI system causes harm, who is responsible: the
developer, the deployer, or the user?

None of this means "don't use AI." It means treat AI output the way you'd
treat any powerful tool wielded by a flawed institution: useful, but never
above scrutiny. The most responsible AI users I know are the ones who stay
skeptical, not the ones who stay away.

That wraps Phase 1 — the fundamentals. Tomorrow we shift into the practical
side: how to actually get great results out of these tools, starting with
prompt engineering.
