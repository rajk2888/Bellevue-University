Day 11/30 — Your first great prompt: anatomy of a good instruction

---

"Write me a marketing email" and "Write a 150-word marketing email for busy
IT managers, announcing our new uptime SLA, in a confident but not
salesy tone, with a single clear call to action" will get you two
completely different quality levels of output. Same model. Same person.
Wildly different results.

A prompt that consistently gets good results usually has four parts:

→ Task — what exactly do you want done? Be specific about the verb:
summarize, compare, rewrite, critique.

→ Context — what does the model need to know that it can't guess? Your
audience, your constraints, relevant background.

→ Format — how should the output look? Length, structure, tone, bullet
points vs prose.

→ Examples (when it matters) — showing one sample of what "good" looks
like often outperforms paragraphs of description. More on this tomorrow.

The mental shift that helped me most: stop treating the AI like a search
engine you ask a question, and start treating it like a new freelancer you
just hired — the more precise your brief, the better the deliverable.
