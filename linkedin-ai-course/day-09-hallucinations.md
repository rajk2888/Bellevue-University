Day 9/30 — Hallucinations: why AI confidently makes things up

---

The single most important thing to understand before you trust AI output
for anything important: it was never optimized to tell the truth. It was
optimized to sound plausible.

Remember Day 6 — an LLM generates the next most statistically likely token,
based on patterns in its training data. Most of the time, "statistically
likely" and "factually true" line up, because accurate text was common in
training. But when the model is asked something obscure, ambiguous, or
outside its training data, it doesn't have a built-in "I don't know" switch
by default — it just keeps generating the most plausible-sounding
continuation. That's a hallucination: fluent, confident, and wrong.

A few patterns that trigger hallucinations more often:
→ Asking for specific citations, statistics, or quotes
→ Questions about very recent events (past the training cutoff)
→ Obscure or niche topics with little training data
→ Leading questions that assume a false premise

The fix isn't to distrust AI entirely — it's to treat it like a brilliant,
overconfident intern: great first draft, always verify the facts that
matter before you rely on them.
